# Stream ordering and flood control in merged multi-source feeds

Scope: how unified timelines order items from many sources, how they keep high-volume sources from flooding, how they handle duplicates, and how they show provenance. Researched 2026-10-02. Topics already covered elsewhere are not repeated here: TweetDeck/Mastodon columns, Reddit view modes, Feedly per-source views, Pinterest smart feed, Tumblr Best Stuff First, and the Tumblr reblog-notes reversal.

## 1. How do unified streams order items? Chronological, best-first, or a hybrid, and where did products back down?

### Takeaway
Products that started as reading tools (RSS readers, GitHub, Mastodon) default to strict chronological order and have reversed experiments that broke it. Meta products keep algorithmic ranking as the default because it raises usage. Chronological views are offered there, but they are second-class and keep getting pulled back. The clearest recent reversal is GitHub, which in February 2025 dropped relevance ordering in its feed after users said it hurt their daily work.

### Cited Findings
- **GitHub feed (2025):** On 2025-02-14 GitHub returned the home-page activity feed to newest-first chronological order. The relevance sort it replaced could place items out of sequence. GitHub said the change came from user feedback that this ordering "can make it difficult to be effective with daily tasks." — [GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-02-14-reverting-feed-activity-sorting-back-to-chronological-ordering/)
- **GitHub home dashboard (2025):** Starting August–October 2025 (public preview), the default home view became short lists of agent tasks, PRs and issues, and the feed moved to its own page at github.com/feed. In effect, the "what needs me" view and the "river" became separate surfaces. — [GitHub Changelog Oct 2025](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-10-28-home-dashboard-update-in-public-preview/); [Aug 2025](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-08-28-improvements-to-the-home-dashboard-available-in-public-preview/)
- **Reeder (Sept 2024 rewrite):** All sources (RSS, YouTube, podcasts, social) go into one "Home" timeline in chronological order. There is no read/unread state and no unread badge. The app saves your scroll position, new items appear above it, and the position syncs across devices. The review was published September 2024. — [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/reeder-a-new-approach-to-following-feeds/); [9to5Mac 2024-09-05](https://9to5mac.com/2024/09/05/reeder-launches-new-app-that-unites-rss-with-video-audio-and-social-feeds/)
- **Reeder backlash:** Some long-time users rejected the merged, mixed-media timeline. Tao of Mac wrote: "I want to _read_ stuff, not wade through a smorgasbord." Criticisms at launch included no sync-service support (iCloud only), no manual folders ("all RSS feeds are lumped together"), and fewer typography settings. The old app stayed available as "Reeder Classic." — [Tao of Mac 2024-09-05](https://taoofmac.com/space/links/2024/09/05/2337); [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/reeder-a-new-approach-to-following-feeds/); [App Store: Reeder Classic](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/reeder-classic/id1529445840)
- **NetNewsWire:** The app has "Today" (roughly the past 24 hours), "All Unread," and "Starred" smart feeds. The timeline is newest-first by default. A single global "Sort Oldest to Newest" setting applies to all feeds. It has no ranking. — [NetNewsWire help: smart feeds](https://netnewswire.com/help/mac/5.1/en/smart-feeds.html); [sorting](https://netnewswire.com/help/ios/5.0/en/sorting-the-timeline.html)
- **Feedbin:** Lists are ordered by publish date, newest first. Users have asked for per-feed or per-view sort order in a long-standing support issue. Search accepts `sort:asc` and `sort:desc`. — [Feedbin search syntax](https://feedbin.com/help/search-syntax/); [feedbin/support #13](https://github.com/feedbin/support/issues/13)
- **Mastodon home:** Strictly reverse-chronological. A boost moves the original post to the top of the feed instead of adding a second copy (details in section 4). — [mastodon PR #21532](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/pull/21532)
- **Instagram (history):** Instagram replaced the chronological feed with ranking in 2016, which caused an immediate backlash. Chronological "Following" and "Favorites" feeds (Favorites capped at 50 accounts) came back on 2022-03-23, but they cannot be set as the default. — [TechCrunch 2022-03-23](https://techcrunch.com/2022/03/23/instagram-launches-chronological-and-favorites-feeds-for-all-users-but-they-cant-be-the-default); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2022/03/23/instagrams-chronological-feed-is-finally-back/)
- **Threads:** Adam Mosseri wrote (March 2024) that making the chronological feed the default makes "people use the app a lot less," and average reach "goes down a lot." In February 2025 Threads let users set Following as the default anyway. Users then reported around 2025-10-21 that the iOS app no longer allowed this and opened to For You. That report comes from a user post, not a Meta announcement. — [Mosseri on Threads](https://www.threads.com/@mosseri/post/C4gk87Xvb_d?hl=en); [Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/tech/meta-ok-ok-you-can-set-your-following-feed-as-the-default-in-threads/); [user report Oct 2025](https://www.threads.com/@mahmoudzitani/post/DQEIwdvDLJO/it-appears-that-threads-has-removed-the-ability-to-set-the-chronological-followi?hl=en)
- **Facebook (2022):** In July 2022 Facebook added a "Feeds" tab that shows friends, groups, Pages and Favorites (up to 30) in chronological order, next to the ranked "Home." — [Meta newsroom Jul 2022](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/07/home-and-feeds-on-facebook/); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2022/07/21/facebook-feeds-tab-friends-favorites/)
- **X Following timeline:** The open-sourced Home Mixer describes "Following" as reverse-chronological. It still goes through the same mixing step as For You (ads, who-to-follow modules, conversation modules for replies). — [twitter/the-algorithm home-mixer README](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm/blob/main/home-mixer/README.md)

### Inferences
- Reading and work tools (GitHub, RSS readers, Mastodon) treat chronological order as the trusted default and pay a cost when they break it. MCPortal's plan to merge by publish time within each tier fits that pattern.
- The "agent's picks" tier is a best-first block inside a chronological river. That works only if the tier is small, clearly labeled, and separate from the time-ordered part, the way Facebook separates Home from Feeds and Threads separates For You from Following. If picks are blended into the time order, users will likely react the way they did to GitHub's relevance sort.
- Reeder's position-based model (no unread count, resume where you left off) is the closest precedent to a "caught up" divider. Its backlash was mostly about losing folders and read state, not about the merged order. MCPortal keeps rooms and portals as per-source views, which answers the Tao of Mac complaint.

### Gaps
- I did not find current documentation on how Tapestry, Readwise Reader's Feed, Inoreader's "All articles," or Bluesky custom feeds order a merged timeline. Tapestry's 1.1 release notes do not describe ordering.
- Tumblr and Reddit home are not repeated here (covered by prior research).

## 2. How do products mix sources with very different volumes and ranking semantics? Are there documented interleaving or balancing rules?

### Takeaway
The best-documented rule is X's open-source "author diversity" heuristic. It does not cap posts. It multiplies each further item from the same author by a geometric decay (0.5) with a floor (0.25). It sits next to explicit "content balance" (in-network vs out-of-network) and "feedback fatigue" heuristics. I found no consumer RSS reader that interleaves or round-robins sources. Readers rely on user tools instead: exclude a source from the merged view, filter it, or mute it.

### Cited Findings
- **X Home Mixer heuristics:** The README lists Author Diversity, Content Balance (in-network vs out-of-network), Feedback fatigue, Deduplication / previously seen removal, and Visibility filtering. A mixing step afterwards inserts ads, who-to-follow modules and prompts. Both timelines use "Conversation Modules for replies." — [home-mixer README](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm/blob/main/home-mixer/README.md); [X engineering blog, 2023-03-31](https://blog.x.com/engineering/en_us/topics/open-source/2023/twitter-recommendation-algorithm)
- **Author-diversity math:** Each successive post from the same author is scored as `score * ((1 - 0.25) * 0.5^position + 0.25)`. The first post keeps its full score, the second gets about 0.625×, the third about 0.44×, and further posts approach 0.25×. Per the source, this is why no single account can take over the feed "no matter how well it scores." Source is a third-party read of the open code. — [frameos.studio](https://frameos.studio/blog/twitter-algorithm); [awesome-twitter-algo](https://github.com/igorbrigadir/awesome-twitter-algo)
- **LinkedIn:** Practitioner guides and a 2026 LinkedIn sequential-recommender paper mention a "diversity score" feature in ranking. I found no public, explicit consecutive-same-author rule. — [arXiv 2602.12354](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12354v1); [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm)
- **Facebook "Show more / Show less" (Oct 2022):** These per-post signals feed the ranking model and last 60 days. Meta said its research showed people want these preferences to last at least a few weeks. — [Meta newsroom Oct 2022](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/10/new-ways-to-customize-your-facebook-feed/); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2022/10/05/facebook-adds-show-show-less-controls-personalize-feed/)
- **Reeder:** By default every feed goes into Home. A per-feed toggle removes a feed from Home while keeping it subscribed. This is the reader's main lever against high-volume feeds. Filters arrived in Reeder 2025.2 (around February 2025). They create custom timelines by including or excluding keywords, media types or feed types; the Audio and Video timelines are built-in filters. Version 2025.5 added a setting to delete old timeline items. — [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/reeder-a-new-approach-to-following-feeds/); [aboutRSS on X](https://x.com/aboutRSS/status/1888964374330523670); [Reeder help](https://reeder.app/help/)
- **Mastodon exclusive lists (4.2.0, Sept 2023):** A list marked "Exclusive" ("Hide these posts from home") keeps its members' posts out of Home, so high-volume accounts can live in their own list. This was "a much-requested feature" (open since issue #9964). — [PR #22048](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/pull/22048); [FediTips](https://mstdn.social/@feditips/111109850392467242); [lists API docs](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/methods/lists/); [issue #9964](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/issues/9964)

### Inferences
- X's decay curve suggests an alternative to MCPortal's hard "fold after 3": a soft penalty. MCPortal's river has no scores, though, so a fold, which moves items rather than reordering them, is the more honest equivalent. The X rule supports the idea that a source's first item should always appear and only later items get demoted.
- Most readers give users "exclude from the merged view" (Reeder, Mastodon exclusive lists) rather than automatic balancing. MCPortal's planned quiet/muffle setting fits this. A three-level setting (normal, folded or muffled, excluded from river but still in its portal) matches known precedents.
- For the scored vs unscored problem (HN points vs RSS), no product I found mixes these by score. Merging by time across sources while keeping each source's internal order, as MCPortal plans, is not contradicted by any source.

### Gaps
- No primary source on Instagram, Threads or LinkedIn hard caps on consecutive items per author.
- No academic HCI paper on per-source caps in RSS-style merged feeds was found within the search budget.

## 3. Flood control features: what exists and what do users like?

### Takeaway
Flood control comes in three forms. **Collapse in place** (Tapestry Muffle, GitHub grouped push events, Techmeme "more" clusters) keeps the item visible but small. **Remove from the merged view** (Reeder "hide from Home," Mastodon exclusive lists, Bluesky or Mastodon hide-reposts per account) moves the source to its own surface. **Filter or mute** (Feedly, Inoreader, Ivory) uses rules. Collapsing tends to be the setting people trust most because nothing silently disappears. Mastodon's bug history shows that hiding items without rebalancing can leave a feed looking empty.

### Cited Findings
- **Tapestry Muffle (launched with 1.0 on 2025-02-04, from Twitterrific):** A muffled item stays in the timeline in a minimized "Mini mode" that can be expanded. A mute removes it entirely. Rules can apply to all timelines or "a specific subset of feeds." They match keywords, wildcards or regex across title, body, links and ALT text, with ALL/ANY logic. Muffles and mutes are a premium feature. — [TechCrunch review 2025-02-04](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/04/a-review-of-tapestry-an-app-powered-by-the-growing-open-web/); [Iconfactory KB](https://iconfactory.happyfox.com/kb/article/161-a-guide-to-creating-muffles-mutes-in-iconfactory-tapestry/); [Tapestry premium](https://iconfactory.happyfox.com/kb/article/152-tapestry-premium-features-and-pricing/)
- **GitHub grouped push events (effective 2025-04-21):** In the org feed, push events were combined into one card (newest first) that expands to show each push, instead of one line per push mixed among other events. — [GitHub Changelog 2025-04-18](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-04-18-performance-improvements-for-the-organizational-feed/)
- **GitHub (2018):** The dashboards gained "interactive grouped events" in the activity feed. — [GitHub Changelog 2018-05-17](https://github.blog/changelog/2018-05-17-user-landingpage-changes/)
- **Mastodon "Show boosts":** There is a global toggle on Home (also covering replies and quotes) and a per-account "Hide boosts from @x." A known problem: boosts hidden on the client still used up slots in each loaded page, so a heavy booster could make the timeline look almost empty or block loading older posts. There is also an open request (#34399) to bring the toggle to lists. — [issue #20658](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/issues/20658); [issue #14957](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/issues/14957); [issue #34399](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/issues/34399); [fedi.tips](https://fedi.tips/does-mastodon-and-the-fediverse-use-algorithms-does-it-start-pushing-stuff-at-me-based-on-my-interactions/)
- **Bluesky Following feed preferences:** Users can turn off reposts, replies and quote posts. Each followed account has "Hide reposts in feeds." Replies in Following are limited to conversations between at least two people the user follows. — [EFF 2024-06](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2024/06/how-clean-your-bluesky-feed); [SocialBee](https://socialbee.com/blog/bluesky-settings/)
- **Feedly Mute Filters:** Mute keywords, companies, people, topics, authors or sites, scoped to a folder, for 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, or forever. — [Feedly docs](https://docs.feedly.com/article/109-how-can-i-add-create-a-mute-filter); [Feedly AI and Mute Filters](https://feedly.com/new-features/posts/feedly-ai-and-mute-filters)
- **Inoreader:** Content filters and duplicate filters are Pro features, with a combined quota of 50 filters as of April 2026. A dashboard shows "how many articles each filter removed today." — [Inoreader blog 2026-04](https://www.inoreader.com/blog/2026/04/new-combined-filter-quotas.html); [automations 2026-01](https://www.inoreader.com/blog/2026/01/save-time-with-automations.html)
- **Ivory (Tapbots):** Quick filters (Media Only, links, boosts only) and saved custom include/exclude keyword filters. Adding someone to a list gives an option to avoid seeing their posts twice (in the timeline and in the list). — [Ivory timeline tips](https://tapbots.com/support/ivory/tips/timeline); [MacStories Ivory review](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/ivory-for-mastodon-review-tapbots-reborn/)
- **X:** Conversation Modules group replies into threads in both For You and Following. "Feedback fatigue" lowers content after negative signals such as "show fewer." — [home-mixer README](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm/blob/main/home-mixer/README.md)

### Inferences
- Tapestry Muffle is the closest precedent for MCPortal's "N more from X" fold: collapsed, expands in place, never hidden silently. MCPortal applies it by run length, where Tapestry uses rules. Both keep a placeholder that shows something was there.
- Mastodon's hidden-boost bug is a direct warning. If the river folds or excludes items after fetching a page, page size has to count the visible items. Otherwise one flooding source can make a page look empty or stop pagination.
- GitHub's grouped push card is very close to a "N more from X" fold: same source, same kind of event, newest first, expandable. GitHub shipped it as an improvement alongside a performance migration.
- Inoreader's "removed today" counter and Feedly's mute expiry are cheap ways to build trust in a future quiet/muffle setting. They show what was suppressed and let the setting lapse.

### Gaps
- I found no user sentiment data (surveys or numbers) on Muffle or collapse-in-place versus outright hiding. The evidence is feature design and issue threads.
- I found no primary documentation for Facebook's "see more from" or Google Reader/Bloglines-era collapsing. History beyond the items above could not be verified within budget.
- Twitter's old "show fewer from this account" UI wording was not verified against a primary source.

## 4. Duplicate handling: the same link or post from several sources

### Takeaway
Two approaches dominate. **Fold the duplicates under one primary item** (NewsBlur story clustering March 2026, Techmeme clusters, Tapestry Crosstalk muffling, Mastodon moving the original up on boost). **Delete them** (Inoreader duplicate filters, Feedly Leo dedup at 85% or more overlap). Folding keeps "who else covered this" visible, which matters when the source is the unit of meaning.

### Cited Findings
- **NewsBlur story clustering (2026-03-18):** Two layers. First, normalized fuzzy title matching on significant-word overlap. Second, semantic matching with Elasticsearch `more_like_this` across all of the user's feeds. The highest-scoring version is shown, and the others "fold neatly underneath" with favicon and feed name in the list, and as rich cards (thumbnail, author, date) in the detail view. Results are cached for 14 days. Auto-marking duplicates read is off by default. — [NewsBlur blog](https://blog.newsblur.com/2026/03/18/story-clustering/); [feature page](https://www.newsblur.com/features/story-clustering)
- **Tapestry Crosstalk (v1.1, 2025-04-08):** It "automatically finds and muffles similar posts across multiple feeds," aimed at people who cross-post to Bluesky, Mastodon and Micro.blog. It detects repeated text, and duplicates are muffled rather than deleted. — [Iconfactory blog](https://blog.iconfactory.com/2025/04/tapestry-whats-new-no-deja-vu/)
- **Inoreader duplicate filters:** Scoped to a feed or folder. Matching is by Same URL (the most common), Same title, or Nearly identical titles. The lookback window runs from 6 hours to 1 month. Matches are removed. — [Inoreader automations 2026-01](https://www.inoreader.com/blog/2026/01/save-time-with-automations.html)
- **Feedly Leo deduplication:** Removes articles with 85% or more content overlap. Articles under 200 words cannot be deduplicated. — [Feedly / coywolf](https://coywolf.com/news/productivity/feedly-pro-plus-leo-ai/); [Feedly Leo](https://futuretools.io/tools/feedly-leo)
- **Techmeme:** Coverage piles up under a lead headline as a cluster, with a dropdown for the extended list. The lead headline is whichever one led when the cluster peaked, "not always" the most representative. — [Techmeme news](https://news.techmeme.com/121231/2012-top-tech-stories); [TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/01/26/the-new-techmeme-design-plus-your-world-minus-underlined-links/)
- **Mastodon home:** The home feed is a Redis sorted set keyed by (reblog_of_id or status_id). A new boost moves the existing post up instead of adding a duplicate. Boosts of a post seen in the last 40 items (REBLOG_FALLOFF) are suppressed. A proposal to widen this to 400 items (PR #21532, opened 2022) was closed unmerged in November 2024 without consensus. Users had complained about seeing boosts of posts they already saw from accounts they follow. — [PR #21532](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/pull/21532); [issue #19293](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/issues/19293)
- **X:** "Deduplication / previously seen Tweets removal" is a listed Home Mixer step. — [home-mixer README](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm/blob/main/home-mixer/README.md)

### Inferences
- For MCPortal, the same URL arriving from RSS, HN and a followed person's share is the common case. A NewsBlur or Tapestry-style fold works best: one card, with "also from HN · shared by @x" attached. It keeps provenance and avoids repeating the card. A URL-equality first pass (Inoreader's most-used mode) is cheap and deterministic. Fuzzy or semantic matching can come later.
- Mastodon's fixed 40-item window shows how fragile a count-based dedup window is. A time-based or seen-state window fits better with MCPortal's unseen/seen tiers.
- Which copy becomes primary is a policy choice. Options include the first seen, the user's own followed share (social context), or the original publisher. Techmeme notes that a mechanical choice is often not the most representative.

### Gaps
- Reeder's and Feedbin's handling of cross-feed duplicates was not documented in the sources found.
- No data on how often users expand folded duplicates.

## 5. How do unified streams keep provenance visible so the source stays the unit of meaning?

### Takeaway
Provenance is carried by per-item source attribution (favicon and feed name, a "reposted by" header) and by a per-source "home" that the merged stream links to. Reeder and Mastodon let a source be removed from the merged view while keeping its own view. NewsBlur shows every alternative source with its favicon and name under a cluster.

### Cited Findings
- NewsBlur clusters list every source under the primary story with favicon and feed name, and as rich cards in detail view. — [NewsBlur blog](https://blog.newsblur.com/2026/03/18/story-clustering/)
- Reeder long-press on a feed opens per-feed settings for name, Home visibility, and display format. Filters create additional timelines alongside Home. — [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/reeder-a-new-approach-to-following-feeds/); [Reeder help](https://reeder.app/help/)
- In Tapestry, feeds can be customized "directly from an item's Actions menu (⋯)." That is, source controls can be reached from an item in the merged stream. — [Iconfactory blog 2025-04](https://blog.iconfactory.com/2025/04/tapestry-whats-new-no-deja-vu/)
- Bluesky and Mastodon both offer per-account repost controls from the profile, so boosted content is attributed to and controlled by the sharer. — [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2024/06/how-clean-your-bluesky-feed); [fedi.tips](https://fedi.tips/does-mastodon-and-the-fediverse-use-algorithms-does-it-start-pushing-stuff-at-me-based-on-my-interactions/)
- GitHub's grouped push card attributes the whole group to one repo and event type. — [GitHub Changelog 2025-04-18](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-04-18-performance-improvements-for-the-organizational-feed/)

### Inferences
- In MCPortal, each river card and each "N more from X" fold should link to that source's portal, and the source's quiet/muffle controls should be one action away from the card, as in Tapestry. The fold label ("N more from X") does double duty as flood control and provenance.
- The planned caught-up divider plus position-based resume (Reeder's model) works with provenance as long as folds remember their state across the divider.

### Gaps
- No design write-up or HCI study was found that measures how source attribution in merged feeds affects comprehension or trust.
- I did not find documentation for how Readwise Reader's Feed displays provenance or ordering.
