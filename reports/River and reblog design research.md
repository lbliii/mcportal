# Run the river finite, reblog by reference

The evidence supports the river plan's core bets: a chronological merge that keeps each source's own order, folding rather than hiding floods, explicit paging rather than infinite scroll inline, and a pill instead of reshuffling. It also points to six changes before the plan ships. **Paging has to count visible units, not fetched items.** Mastodon learned that hiding items after fetching a page can leave a timeline looking empty. **Duplicates should be clustered across the whole river session and against what the user has already seen**, not inside a fixed-count window, which Mastodon's 40-item rule shows is fragile. **The iframe needs an explicit height write after every render**, because Claude.ai ignores the MCP Apps `size-changed` notification and reads DOM height instead. **Fullscreen auto-load needs a cap.** **Reading position should be a server-side item-ID marker**, not a scroll offset. **The feed needs real ARIA feed semantics.** For reblogging, a decade of platform history favors a reference-based model over Tumblr's copies. That model credits both the root sharer and the person you got it from (Bluesky's `via`), offers an optional rather than forced note (forcing one cut sharing about 20% and produced one-word filler), gives authors Mastodon-style consent controls, and shows only first-hop reblogs from people you follow, because misinformation concentrates at reshare depth 2 and beyond. The reblog icon should keep the near-universal rectangular two-arrow loop, clearly unlike MCPortal's circular orbit refresh icon. Its brand twist is the Line mark's moon filling the centre of the loop when active. That gives a state signal that doesn't rely on colour, paired with an ink green and a visible "Reblog" label. Two weaknesses run through the evidence. Almost none of it measures feeds inside chat-embedded iframes, and the multi-follow dedupe rule for reblogs is a design synthesis rather than a documented practice.

## Chronology is the trusted default, and picks must stay a fenced block

Reading and work tools treat newest-first as a contract, and they pay when they break it. GitHub reverted its feed to chronological order in February 2025 after users said relevance sorting "can make it difficult to be effective with daily tasks" ([GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-02-14-reverting-feed-activity-sorting-back-to-chronological-ordering/)). NetNewsWire and Feedbin sort by date with no ranking ([NetNewsWire](https://netnewswire.com/help/mac/5.1/en/smart-feeds.html); [Feedbin](https://feedbin.com/help/search-syntax/)). Reeder's 2024 rewrite merged every source into one chronological Home timeline, and the backlash was about lost folders and read state, not the merge ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/reeder-a-new-approach-to-following-feeds/); [Tao of Mac](https://taoofmac.com/space/links/2024/09/05/2337)). MCPortal keeps portals as per-source views, which answers that complaint directly. The ranked platforms show the other side: Adam Mosseri said a chronological default makes "people use the app a lot less" ([Threads](https://www.threads.com/@mosseri/post/C4gk87Xvb_d?hl=en)), and Instagram's chronological feeds can't be the default ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/03/23/instagram-launches-chronological-and-favorites-feeds-for-all-users-but-they-cant-be-the-default)). MCPortal isn't optimizing for usage minutes, so the reader-tool precedent is the right one.

No product found blends heterogeneous scores across sources. X's open-source Home Mixer is the best-documented balancing rule. It decays each further post from one author by `0.5^position` toward a 0.25 floor, so the first post always keeps its full weight ([home-mixer README](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm/blob/main/home-mixer/README.md); [frameos analysis](https://frameos.studio/blog/twitter-algorithm)). The river has no scores, so a fold is the honest equivalent of that decay: the first items from a source always show, and later ones move into a placeholder. The plan's merge (take the head of whichever portal queue is newest, preserving each portal's internal rank) holds up against everything researched. The agent's picks are the one best-first element. They work only because they sit above the chronological part as a small, labelled block. That is the Home-versus-Feeds split Facebook made ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/07/home-and-feeds-on-facebook/)). If they leaked into the time order, users would likely react the way GitHub's did. The plan already does this, and it should stay that way.

### Duplicates belong in a cluster that remembers what you saw

Two dedupe philosophies compete. Inoreader removes matches by same URL (its most-used mode), same title or near-identical title, within a 6-hour to 1-month lookback ([Inoreader](https://www.inoreader.com/blog/2026/01/save-time-with-automations.html)). Feedly removes articles with 85% or more content overlap ([coywolf](https://coywolf.com/news/productivity/feedly-pro-plus-leo-ai/)). The other camp folds duplicates under one primary item. NewsBlur's March 2026 clustering shows the best version and lists the others "neatly underneath" with favicon and feed name, and it leaves auto-marking duplicates read off by default ([NewsBlur](https://blog.newsblur.com/2026/03/18/story-clustering/)). Tapestry's Crosstalk muffles cross-posts rather than deleting them ([Iconfactory](https://blog.iconfactory.com/2025/04/tapestry-whats-new-no-deja-vu/)). Folding is right for MCPortal, because "who else carried this" is provenance, and the plan's "Simon Willison · also on Hacker News" is exactly the NewsBlur pattern.

The plan's weak spot is scope. It dedupes within one fetch. Mastodon's home feed dedupes boosts only against the last **40** items, and a proposal to widen that to 400 was closed unmerged after years of complaints about re-seeing posts ([PR #21532](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/pull/21532); [issue #19293](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/issues/19293)). A count-based window fails as volume changes. MCPortal already has seen-state, which is the better window. Cluster by normalized URL across everything the river holds in a session (new, seen and later pages), and when a URL the user has already seen arrives from a new portal or a follow's share, attach it to the earlier card's "also on" tail instead of surfacing it as new. Techmeme's experience is that mechanically chosen lead items are "not always" the most representative ([Techmeme](https://news.techmeme.com/121231/2012-top-tech-stories)). So choose the primary deliberately: position goes to the earliest occurrence (as planned), the from-line names the original publisher, and a follow's share supplies the social-context row above it. URL equality first; fuzzy or semantic matching can wait.

## Folding works if pages count what the reader actually sees

Flood control comes in three forms, and the trusted one is collapse in place. Tapestry's Muffle keeps an item visible in an expandable mini mode, while Mute removes it ([Iconfactory KB](https://iconfactory.happyfox.com/kb/article/161-a-guide-to-creating-muffles-mutes-in-iconfactory-tapestry/)). GitHub's April 2025 org feed folds a repo's pushes into one expandable newest-first card ([GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-04-18-performance-improvements-for-the-organizational-feed/)). Removal from the merged view is the second form: Reeder's per-feed "hide from Home" and Mastodon's 2023 exclusive lists move a noisy source to its own surface ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/reeder-a-new-approach-to-following-feeds/); [Mastodon PR #22048](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/pull/22048)). The plan's "7 more from r/programming" is the GitHub grouped card applied by run length, and the fold label does double duty as provenance.

The decisive warning is Mastodon's hidden-boost bug. Boosts hidden on the client still used up slots in each loaded page, so a heavy booster could make the timeline look almost empty or block loading older posts ([issue #20658](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/issues/20658); [issue #14957](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/issues/14957)). The river folds after merging, so "10 more" must mean ten *visible units*, where a fold row counts as one unit. Otherwise a page drawn from a busy subreddit becomes three cards and a fold. Folded items still count toward "N new", as the plan says, and are marked seen only when expanded on screen. The fold's expansion state must survive redraws and the caught-up divider.

For the later quiet setting, precedent supports three levels: normal, quiet (fold after one) and "keep out of the river" while still living in its portal (Reeder's toggle, Mastodon's exclusive lists). Two cheap trust builders exist. Inoreader shows "how many articles each filter removed today" ([Inoreader](https://www.inoreader.com/blog/2026/04/new-combined-filter-quotas.html)), and Feedly lets mutes expire after a day, week or month ([Feedly docs](https://docs.feedly.com/article/109-how-can-i-add-create-a-mute-filter)). Tapestry also puts feed controls in an item's action menu ([Iconfactory](https://blog.iconfactory.com/2025/04/tapestry-whats-new-no-deja-vu/)), so "Quiet this portal" should sit in each card's overflow menu, one step from the flood.

## The post card: context row, from-line, picture, title, actions

Every product studied keeps provenance in one compact line and separates "who made it" from "how it reached you". Tumblr's NPF stores a reblog trail ordered root to parent, with a blog header per layer. Deleted sources keep a `broken_blog_name` so the attribution still renders ([Tumblr NPF](https://www.tumblr.com/docs/npf)). Mastodon's September 2026 status redesign replaced the prepended "X boosted" text with a dedicated, background-styled boost component above the post ([PR #40555](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/pull/40555)). Tumblr's 2023 header experiment cut redundant avatars and went full width to save vertical space ([Changes on Tumblr](https://changes.tumblr.com/post/726375529346973696/tuesday-august-22nd-2023)). Hacker News is the minimal baseline: title, domain, then one subtext line of points, author, age and comments ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/)).

Applied to the `post` form, social context ("@handle shared", later "@handle reblogged from @other") gets its own small, muted row *above* the from-line, styled as a component rather than run-in text. The from-line (portal dot, title button, time) stays the HN-style source line, with "· also on Hacker News" as a tail after the time. Store a plain-text portal and handle name in each item, on Tumblr's `broken_blog_name` model, so the card survives a removed portal or deleted account. "Why this" stays a compact line, not another header row. Tumblr's experience is that recommendation labels compete for the same scarce slot.

Media rules have moved toward native aspect ratios. Twitter dropped its saliency crop in 2021 after its own study found bias, concluding that cropping "is a decision best made by people" ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Business/twitter-scraps-image-cropping-algorithm-allegations-racial-bias/story?id=77801064)). Bluesky stores an `aspectRatio` per image so clients can reserve space ([atproto lexicon](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/blob/main/lexicons/app/bsky/embed/images.json)) and settled link cards at **1.91:1** after tall crops clipped text-heavy OG images ([social-app #2555](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/issues/2555)). Google Discover asks for 16:9 at 1200px or wider and warns against logos and text-heavy images ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)). Most river items will have only an `og:image`. Render it at 1.91:1 (about 640×335 in a 640px column), reserve that box before the thumbnail resolves so the auto-height iframe doesn't jump, and drop images that are site logos, since the portal dot already brands the card.

On text, Baymard finds descriptions wider than 80 characters per line were skipped **41% more often** than those at 60–70, and recommends about 70ch or 34em ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability)). A 640px column at 16px body text runs past 75 characters, so inset the summary to about 34em or set it slightly larger. A four-line clamp then holds roughly 250–300 characters, about one RSS description. Tumblr truncates at block boundaries in data (`truncate_after`) rather than by CSS ([NPF](https://www.tumblr.com/docs/npf)). That suits multi-paragraph share notes. Plain summaries can use `line-clamp`.

For the action row, Mastodon's keyboard model is the de facto standard: j/k to move, Enter or o to open, b to boost, ? for help ([fedi.tips](https://fedi.tips/using-mastodon-through-a-keyboard/)). Adopt that, with s for save and b reserved for reblog. Rare actions (copy link, quiet this portal, report) go in an overflow menu, which Tumblr added to every reblog-trail header in 2023 ([Tumblr support](https://support.tumblr.com/post/733358258142707712)). Targets need at least **24×24 CSS px** under WCAG 2.2 AA ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). The existing `--mp-control-minimum: 24px` and `--mp-control-touch: 44px` tokens cover that. For the print look, Material's outlined card (a 1dp rule, no shadow, 16dp padding) is closest ([Material Components](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-components-android/master/docs/components/Card.md)). In a single reading column, hairline rules between posts, rather than boxed cards, read as print and save height. Keep the plan's "share" (send elsewhere, the up-arrow box) visually distinct from reblog (post to my followers). Instagram's 2025 repost adoption kept them as separate glyphs ([UK CCD](https://ccd.uky.edu/news/instagram-adds-reposts-map-and-new-friends-tab-their-social-media-platform)).

## A finite, accessible river inside a host-scrolled iframe

### Load more beats infinite scroll for a stream people return to

NN/g limits infinite scroll to goal-less browsing and lists its costs: no landmarks for refinding, an illusion of completeness, unreachable footers, keyboard barriers, and lost position on Back ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/infinite-scrolling-tips/)). Baymard's testing found "Load more" carries "probably the smallest cognitive load possible for on-demand loading". Its recommended batches are 10–30 lazy-loaded items for category lists, then a button after 50–100, and 15–30 per batch on mobile ([Smashing/Baymard](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2016/03/pagination-infinite-scrolling-load-more-buttons/)). Google removed continuous scroll from search starting in June 2024 in favour of a "More results" button and pagination, citing no significant gain in satisfaction ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/computing/search-engines/google-confirms-a-major-change-to-search-that-undoes-a-2-year-old-decision)). Etsy's infinite-scroll test cut clicks and favourites from search ([danwin.com](https://danwin.com/2013/01/infinite-scroll-fail-etsy/)). A river with a seen boundary is closer to search than to TikTok, so the plan's inline "10 more" is right.

Ten sits at the low end of Baymard's range. Inline it is defensible: every tap grows the chat transcript, and NN/g's eyetracking put **57% of viewing time on the first screenful** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)). The changes are elsewhere. Count visible units, as covered above. Raise fullscreen batches to about 20, where the transcript cost disappears. **Cap fullscreen auto-load** at two or three batches (roughly 50 units) before reverting to the button, which is Baymard's hybrid and keeps the end line reachable. Inline, offer "Open the full river" once three pages are showing. That keeps a long session out of the transcript. It is a judgment call, since no study covers chat-embedded feeds.

### End states are now a regulatory direction, not just manners

Instagram's "You're All Caught Up" (July 2018) fires after every post from the last 48 hours is seen ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/introducing-youre-all-caught-up-in-feed)), then keeps showing more below it. In 2026 the European Commission preliminarily found TikTok (February) and Meta (July) in breach of the DSA over infinite scroll and autoplay, with remedies including turning infinite scroll off by default ([EP Think Tank](https://epthinktank.eu/2026/05/06/addictive-design-on-online-platforms/); [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320180/20260711/eu-charges-meta-addictive-design-infinite-scroll-violates-dsa-health-rules.htm)). New York's SAFE for Kids Act targets feeds "recommended, selected or prioritized" from persistent user data, and its final rules arrived in July 2026 ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/new-york-attorney-general-releases-final-rules-for-safe-for-kids-act)). These are preliminary findings and youth-specific rules, but the direction is clear. A follow-based, chronological river with a hard end sits on the right side of it. The plan's two-stage end (a divider between unseen and seen, then a true end line) is stronger than Instagram's soft cue. Make the end line literal, in house voice with a plain line after: "Signal ends here. That's everything your 12 portals fetched since Tuesday."

### The reader's position belongs to the reader

Twitter's web timeline stopped auto-inserting because tweets "would often disappear from view mid-read", and new tweets now wait behind a counter bar ([zeno.zone](https://zeno.zone/blog/twitter-scroll-back)). Bluesky users call the feed position "sacred" ([social-app #4107](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/issues/4107)). Under web.dev's CLS rules, content inserted by a background poll is an unexpected shift, while one inserted within 500ms of a click is excluded ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/cls)). The plan's "4 new since you started" pill is correct as written. Two refinements follow. WCAG 2.2.2 treats news-style auto-updates as content that needs a pause, stop or frequency control, with no five-second exemption ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). So check for new items on open and on refocus rather than on a timer, and announce the pill's count politely and only when it changes. Position memory also has to be semantic. Mastodon's markers API stores `last_read_id` plus a version and returns `409 Conflict` on stale writes, so several clients share one position ([Mastodon docs](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/methods/markers/)). Inline, the host owns the scroll offset, so a pixel position means nothing. Persist a marker per room, anchor the caught-up divider to that item ID, and render "where I left off" from it.

### Feed semantics and focus

The WAI-ARIA feed pattern specifies `role="feed"` with `<article>` children carrying `aria-posinset` and `aria-setsize` (−1 when unknown), `aria-busy` during loads, and Page Up/Down between articles with Ctrl+End escaping past the feed. It also warns that keyboard documentation must be discoverable ([W3C APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/feed/)). Screen-reader support for the pattern is uneven and untested here, so treat it as progressive. BBC GEL's load-more puts "Loading, please wait" in a live region, then moves focus to a `role="separator"` announcing the new range, so the next item is next in focus order ([BBC GEL](https://bbc.github.io/gel/components/load-more/)). For the river, the caught-up divider is a separator, not an article. "10 more" focuses a separator reading "Posts 11 to 20". The end line is followed by a real focusable element, so Ctrl+End has a target. Expanding a fold replaces the fold row, so moving focus to the first revealed card (as planned) is right, since the focused element disappears. In-place "read more" on a summary should instead keep focus on its toggle with `aria-expanded`. Gate any smooth scroll-to-new and pill motion behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### The iframe height workaround is mandatory

The MCP Apps spec lets views report `ui/notifications/size-changed`, and says hosts "MUST" honour it ([ext-apps spec](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/ext-apps/blob/main/specification/2026-01-26/apps.mdx)). An open issue reports that Claude.ai ignores it, reads `documentElement` height from the DOM, and can lock at a pre-render height when ResizeObserver fires early. The workaround is to set `document.documentElement.style.height` to the content's `scrollHeight` after each render, and to use a fixed `min-height` instead of `100vh`, which goes circular in an auto-sized iframe ([claude-ai-mcp #69](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-ai-mcp/issues/69)). Goose reportedly shrinks apps and never grows them back ([goose #6818](https://github.com/block/goose/issues/6818)). Every river change that alters height (a page, a fold expansion, the pill redraw, a late thumbnail) should therefore send the spec notification *and* write the explicit height. OpenAI's guidelines forbid nested scrolling in inline cards ([OpenAI Apps SDK](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/concepts/ui-guidelines)), which the plan respects. If a host does impose a fixed inline height, set `overscroll-behavior: contain` on the iframe document's `html` and `body`. MDN notes it has no effect on the `<iframe>` element itself ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/overscroll-behavior)). Fullscreen is the one mode where the view owns scrolling, and so the one place where the APG's Page Up/Down contract and capped auto-load apply.

## Reblog as attributed curation, with consent and a depth limit

### Reference the original, keep a tombstone, pool the credit

Tumblr copies a post and its trail into every reblog so a dashboard load costs one query, even for trails thousands deep ([Tumblr Engineering](https://www.tumblr.com/engineering/189455858864/how-reblogs-work)). The price is that edits never reach existing reblogs, deleting the original removes nothing, and reblogs "can continue being reblogged indefinitely" ([Tumblr Engineering Q&A](https://www.tumblr.com/engineering/189457776594/about-the-how-reblogs-work-post-i-apologize)). Mastodon and Bluesky reference the original, which is what makes author removal and detaching possible. For a small, safety-minded community, reference wins: store `root`, `via` (the share the user saw it in) and the reblogger as separate fields, render live from the root, and keep a snapshot of the reblogger's own note so a deleted root becomes a tombstone ("removed by its author") rather than a broken card. Bluesky added exactly this `via` reference to repost records so apps can credit the curator in the middle of a chain ([hypercerts #255](https://github.com/hypercerts-org/hypercerts-lexicon/issues/255)). In July 2025 it added "reposts of your reposts" notifications ([Bluesky](https://bsky.social/about/blog/07-02-2025-more-notification-control)).

Credit is the most sensitive part. On 16 March 2026 Tumblr gave each reblog its own notes. It drew **more than 100,000 complaints** and reversed in about 30 hours, admitting it "should have communicated this differently" ([PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/03/18/tumblr-reverses-reblog-notes-update-backlash/); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2026/04/16/tumblrs-30-hour-attribution-riot/)). One post fell from 152,372 notes to about 2.8k in the new view ([PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/03/17/tumblr-users-slam-new-reblog-ui-update-own-notes/)). The lesson is that counts pool on the root and credit flows to the original sharer, with per-reblog counts as at most a secondary figure. Any later change to attribution rules gets announced ahead of time.

### One tap, an optional note, and a nudge to read first

Twitter's October 2020 experiment made the retweet button open a quote composer. Sharing fell about **20%**, and the extra quotes added no context: **45% were a single word** and 70% were under 25 characters. Twitter reverted ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2020/12/17/twitter-removes-quote-tweet-prompt/)). The "read before you retweet" prompt, by contrast, raised article opens **40%** ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/twitter-prompt-read-article-before-tweeting-191907421.html)). Bluesky, Threads and Substack put plain repost and quote behind one icon in a small menu ([Bluesky RepostButton.tsx](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/components/PostControls/RepostButton.tsx); [Threads](https://www.threads.com/@threads/post/DFYVYVVRgFy); [Substack](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/14671398684308-What-is-a-restack-on-Substack-Notes)). The MCPortal flow follows from that. Tapping Reblog opens a menu with "Reblog" (posts immediately) and "Reblog with a note" (opens the composer), plus Cancel. Once reblogged, the first item reads "Undo reblog". If the user hasn't opened the item in the reader, a one-line nudge offers "Read it first?" without blocking. MCPortal can know this, which most platforms can't. Wording matters too: Pinterest's rename of "Pin it" to "Save" lifted saves 8% ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/kathleenchaykowski/2016/06/02/pinterest-renames-pin-it-button-as-save/)). Are.na's "Connect" files a block into your own channel and shows everywhere else it lives ([Are.na](https://help.are.na/docs/getting-started/connections)). Framing a reblog as public curation onto your shelf fits a reading platform better than an amplification button.

### Consent controls and the depth limit

Rochko kept quotes out of Mastodon for years because quoting "inevitably adds toxicity" ([Mastodon](https://mastodon.social/@Gargron/99662106175542726)). Mastodon 4.5 added them on consent terms. Authors pick Anyone, Followers only or Just me per post, with a default. Followers-only posts can't be quoted. Authors are notified and can remove their post from a quote. "Quiet public" quotes stay out of search and trends ([Mastodon blog](https://blog.joinmastodon.org/2025/09/introducing-quote-posts/); [fedi.tips](https://fedi.tips/why-cant-i-quote-other-posts-in-mastodon/)). Bluesky's 2024 release let authors detach their post from quotes to limit dogpiling, and records removals publicly ([Bluesky](https://bsky.social/about/blog/08-28-2024-anti-toxicity-features)). Tumblr's 2022 Reblog Control offers Anyone or No One, editable after posting ([Tumblr](https://unwrapping.tumblr.com/post/686005440496173056/reblog-controls)). MCPortal should adopt the union: a per-share "Who can reblog: Anyone, Followers, Nobody" with an account default; reblogs inherit the original's audience ceiling; "Remove my post from this reblog"; blocks checked both at reblog time and at render; and mute or block suppressing the notifications they would cause.

Depth is the lever with the strongest evidence. Facebook's leaked 2019 research found content at reshare depth 2 or more was about **4×** as likely to be misinformation, up to **10×** deeper. It estimated that deep-reshare controls would cut political link misinformation by 25% ([TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-12-facebook-harmless-leaked-documents-misinformation.html)). WhatsApp's 2020 one-chat limit on frequently forwarded messages cut them **70%** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/27/whatsapps-new-limit-cuts-virality-of-highly-forwarded-messages-by-70)). So the river shows only depth 1: a reblog by someone you follow, never a stranger's reblog of a reblog. The full `via` chain stays visible on the post. When several follows reblog the same root, show one card with "Reblogged by Ana, Ben and 2 more". No platform documents this rule, but it follows from the URL-clustering logic above, and Mastodon's move-the-original-up behaviour is the nearest precedent.

## Keep the loop square and put the moon inside it

Almost every platform uses the same reshare glyph: two opposing arrows forming a rectangular loop. That includes Twitter/X, Tumblr, Bluesky, Threads, Substack, and Instagram since 2025 ([Bluesky Repost.tsx](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/components/icons/Repost.tsx); [shadcn mdi-tumblr-reblog](https://www.shadcn.io/icon/mdi-tumblr-reblog); [Minter.io](https://minter.io/blog/how-to-use-the-new-instagram-repost-feature/)). The verbs carry the brand (reblog, boost, restack, repost), not the shape. The ambiguity is with refresh. Circular arrows (Material `cached`, `autorenew`, Lucide `refresh-cw`, Phosphor `ArrowsClockwise`) read as reload or sync ([Material](https://github.com/google/material-design-icons/blob/master/symbols/web/cached/materialsymbolsoutlined/cached_24px.svg); [Lucide](https://github.com/lucide-icons/lucide/blob/main/icons/refresh-cw.svg)). Mastodon's 5.0 redesign is moving its boost to exactly that circular shape ([PR #40655](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/pull/40655)), which makes it a cautionary outlier. MCPortal's house refresh icon is already a circular trip around a tilted orbit (`orbitArrow` in `scripts/brand.ts`), and every portal header carries it. The reblog icon must therefore stay rectangular, so the two never read alike. Lucide's `repeat-2`, explicitly tagged retweet and repost, puts its arrowheads on the vertical legs. Its media-player sibling `repeat` puts them on the horizontal ends ([Lucide repeat-2](https://github.com/lucide-icons/lucide/blob/main/icons/repeat-2.svg); [Lucide repeat](https://github.com/lucide-icons/lucide/blob/main/icons/repeat.svg)). Choose the vertical-arrowhead topology, so the glyph also stays clear of "loop playback". Bluesky and Mastodon use the horizontal version, though, and in an action row next to comment and save either one reads as reshare.

The construction should follow the house set: the 24-unit grid, the 1.75 stroke, round caps, and the house corner radius of 3. That way it sits beside the bookmark and comment icons without looking imported. The brand twist goes **inside the loop**, where Mastodon already signals state. Its boosted icon adds a solid square in the loop's empty centre, and its redesign adds a filled disc ([repeat_active.svg](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/blob/main/app/javascript/svg-icons/repeat_active.svg); [boost_active.svg](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/blob/main/app/javascript/svg-icons/boost_active.svg)). MCPortal's Line mark ends in a small filled moon, and the icon schema already supports a filled `dot`. When idle, the loop is empty. When reblogged, the moon fills the centre: something has passed through the portal. This change in shape satisfies the WCAG rule that colour must not be the only cue. It also holds at the 16px `--mp-icon-normal` size, where a tiny arch would blur into a smudge, so test the dot radius at 16px before committing. A variant with a diagonal slash, like Bluesky's `RepostStrike`, marks "reblogs off" on posts whose author disallowed them.

Colour follows convention. Twitter/X, Tumblr and Bluesky all turn the reshare green, and Bluesky uses its "positive" token ([RepostButton.tsx](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/components/PostControls/RepostButton.tsx)). Mastodon's in-button blue is the outlier, and even Mastodon uses green for boost notifications ([components.scss](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/blob/main/app/javascript/styles/mastodon/components.scss)). The Atomic palette has no pure green, but `--mp-status-success` (#126B57 light, #66D4B7 dark) is a printed-ink green that keeps the association. It sits very close to `--mp-action-primary`, `--mp-text-link` and `--mp-focus-ring` (#1D6B63). Give reblog its own `--mp-action-reblogged` token, so its colour can be tuned toward a warmer, more clearly green ink without moving links, and let the moon dot do the real work of signalling state. For the print-era touch, on activation briefly offset a second-colour copy of the loop by a pixel, like an ink stamp landing slightly off register. Gate it behind `prefers-reduced-motion`, the way Mastodon gates its favourite animation, and don't animate on undo.

The label and its semantics matter as much as the glyph. NN/g finds only home, print and search icons are universally recognized, and calls for visible text labels ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)). Show "Reblog" beside the icon wherever the row has room: fullscreen, and cards 600px or wider. Keep icon plus count only at the narrowest inline width. The button opens a menu, so it is a menu button (`aria-haspopup`), not an `aria-pressed` toggle. MDN warns against changing a toggle's label when its state changes ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Reference/Attributes/aria-pressed)). Follow Bluesky instead: put state and count in the accessible name, "Reblog (12 reblogs)" or "Undo reblog (12 reblogs)", and keep the SVG decorative ([Sara Soueidan](https://www.sarasoueidan.com/blog/accessible-icon-buttons/)). Pulp voice belongs in the menu flavour and the toast ("Sent through the portal!" followed by "Reblogged to your followers."). Accessible names stay plain.

## Conclusion

The research sharpens what kind of thing the river is. It isn't a social feed with reading attached. It is a reader's stream, where the regulatory and usability evidence of the last two years rewards being finite, chronological and explicit about where it ends. Those were MCPortal's instincts already. The real risks are mechanical and easy to miss: pages that count fetched items instead of visible ones, a dedupe window that forgets, and an iframe that stops growing in the one host that matters most. Each costs a few lines to prevent and a confusing week to debug after launch.

Reblogging is where MCPortal can do better than its model. Tumblr's March 2026 reversal shows the community values credit to the original sharer more than any engagement mechanic, and Tumblr's copy model makes consent controls almost impossible to add later. Building on references with `via` attribution, author consent and a depth-1 river from the start gives MCPortal Tumblr's culture without Tumblr's technical debt. The icon carries the same idea at small scale: keep the shape everyone already knows, and make the moon inside the loop show that something has passed through.
