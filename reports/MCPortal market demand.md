# Developers crave feed dashboards; few will pay

There is real demand for the core of MCPortal: one place for the sources you choose, arranged as panels, set up without hand-editing config files. But that demand comes mostly from developers and enthusiasts who use free tools and seldom pay. The mass-market version, AI deciding what you read, has failed again and again. The clearest demand signal is Glance, a self-hosted "all your feeds in one place" dashboard. It went from nothing in April 2024 to **about 37k GitHub stars by September 2026**, passing Homepage, Dashy and the 14-year-old FreshRSS. The loudest complaint in those communities is setup and upkeep, and that is exactly what an agent that composes panels removes. The business evidence is much weaker. The best-known consumer feed company, Feedly, is estimated at only **about $7M ARR**. Big companies keep leaving the category: iGoogle in 2013, Netvibes and Pocket in 2025. AI-native curation has a poor record too: **Artifact shut down after about 444k lifetime downloads**, and **OpenAI retired ChatGPT Pulse after nine months**. The MCP Apps channel is technically ready across Claude, ChatGPT, VS Code and Goose. Commercially it is unproven: there is no in-host billing for digital subscriptions, no usage analytics, and discovery depends on directory listings and model suggestions. The honest verdict is that MCPortal has a credible niche but not yet a proven market. The best wedge is technical power readers who already live in Claude or ChatGPT and want a persistent, source-transparent HN/GitHub/RSS/YouTube workspace. The biggest risks are distribution that depends on the host platforms and a low ceiling on willingness to pay.

## Glance's 37k stars prove the itch, not the wallet

The strongest evidence for MCPortal's exact concept comes from self-hosting. Glance describes itself as a "self-hosted dashboard that puts all your feeds in one place," with panels for RSS, subreddits, YouTube, GitHub releases and Hacker News. It reached **37,278 stars in about 29 months**, ahead of Homepage (32,923), Dashy (26,599) and FreshRSS (16,187), according to a live GitHub API pull on 2026-09-30. Karakeep, a read-later tool created in February 2024, grew to 29,365 stars over the same period, when commercial read-later apps were shutting down. The 2025 selfh.st survey of 4,081 self-hosters had respondents name their favorite apps in free text. FreshRSS came in at #10, Homepage #16, Miniflux #20, Glance #24 and Karakeep #25 among more than 300 apps named ([selfh.st](https://selfh.st/survey/2025-results/)). Tech press treats Glance as something to make your home page ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/glance-is-a-beautiful-self-hosted-dashboard-that-ive-already-set-as-my-web-browsers-home-page/)). A 2026 PC Gamer piece titled "Kill the algorithm in your head" shows anti-algorithm RSS framing reaching mainstream outlets ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/kill-the-algorithm-in-your-head-lets-set-up-rss-readers-and-get-news-we-actually-want-in-2026/)).

What people ask for lines up closely with MCPortal's design. In the Glance Show HN thread (211 points), the most praised feature was the preset: "The preconfigured page they provide as an example is almost exactly like I would want it to be configured." The main complaints were upkeep and closed platforms: "I'll get it set up just so, then some api changes," and "an insanely expensive api, cough reddit" ([HN 40357611](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40357611)). A late-2025 "Is RSS Still Alive?" thread found users relying on RSS for YouTube, blogs and GitHub. They complained that feeds exist but are hard to discover, and a vocal group rejected AI curation: "You can't replace a pipe with a concierge" ([HN 46309919](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46309919)). These map onto MCPortal features. Starter packs answer the preset praise. Agent composition answers the YAML upkeep. Source discovery answers the hard-to-find feeds. Reader view with the agent arranging rather than rewriting answers the anti-summary camp.

The commercial side of the category is much thinner than the stars suggest. Syndicated reports put the RSS reader market at **$322–450M** growing about 7% a year ([Verified Market Research](https://www.verifiedmarketresearch.com/product/really-simple-syndication-rss-reader-market/); [Market Research Intellect](https://www.marketresearchintellect.com/product/global-really-simple-syndication-rss-reader-market-size-and-forecast/)). None of them discloses a methodology, and a bottom-up check contradicts them. Feedly, the largest player, is estimated at **$7.3M ARR** and bootstrapped ([GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/feedly.com)). Momentum, the best-known new-tab dashboard, reported about **$996K ARR** with a single founder in 2024 ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/momentum-dashboard-breakdown)). Consumer feed-reading software is realistically a market of tens of millions of dollars, not hundreds of millions.

Large companies also keep leaving. Dassault retired Netvibes on June 2, 2025, citing a declining user base and rising maintenance costs ([DashDork](https://dashdork.com/blog/netvibes-shutdown-alternatives/)). Mozilla closed Pocket on July 8, 2025 because "the way people are browsing the web is changing" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/read-it-later-app-pocket-is-shutting-down-here-are-the-best-alternatives/)). Omnivore shut down in November 2024 after an acqui-hire ([Gleamr](https://gleamr.io/blog/omnivore-shut-down-alternatives)). Displaced users moved to small indie products and self-hosted tools. Readwise Reader's user base reportedly **roughly quadrupled from June to August 2025**, though that figure comes only secondhand ([LabHub](https://labhub.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-rss-readers-read-later-2026-reeder-netnewswire-inoreader-feedbin-miniflux-freshrss-karakeep-deep-dive?lang=en)). The pattern is lasting enthusiast demand that has been burned repeatedly, now spread across indies, and that cares a great deal about export and portability.

## AI-chosen feeds keep dying; user-steered sources survive

Every high-profile attempt to have AI decide what people read has stalled. Artifact, built by Instagram's founders, had about 100k downloads near launch but only about 12k new installs in October 2023, and **about 444k lifetime**. It shut down because the "market opportunity isn't big enough," and its technology went to Yahoo ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/18/why-artifact-from-instagrams-founders-failed-shut-down/); [Yahoo](https://www.yahooinc.com/press/yahoo-announces-the-acquisition-of-artifact-the-news-discovery-platform-created-by-instagram-cofounders-kevin-systrom-and-mike-krieger)). TechCrunch's post-mortem blamed feature creep (link sharing, posts, place recommendations, image generation) and noted that chatbots now summarize news without a separate app.

ChatGPT Pulse is the most instructive case because it is the closest thing to MCPortal that a host platform has shipped. It launched on 2025-09-25 as a Pro-only ($200/month) proactive morning brief ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/openai-launches-chatgpt-pulse-to-proactively-write-you-morning-briefs)) and never reached Plus. On 2026-06-17 OpenAI announced it would retire Pulse in favor of scheduled tasks. OpenAI said it saw "strong engagement with tasks within the Pulse interface," and that proactive experiences work best when "personalised, action-oriented, and steerable by the user" ([Digit](https://www.digit.in/news/general/openai-is-retiring-chatgpt-pulse-and-replacing-it-with-scheduled-tasks-here-is-why.html)). Google followed the same path. Its Labs "CC" email briefing in December 2025 ([Google](https://blog.google/technology/google-labs/cc-ai-agent/)) became Gemini Daily Brief at I/O 2026 and was then opened to free US users ([Chrome Unboxed](https://chromeunboxed.com/google-is-rolling-out-gemini-daily-brief-to-free-users-dropping-the-paid-ai-requirement/)), with signs of a "Tuning" control for steering it ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/ai/daily-brief-tuning-could-be-what-google-has-planned-next-for-geminis-morning-digest)).

These cases cut two ways for MCPortal. On one side, the platforms' own lesson supports its model: the user names the sources and the agent assembles them, which is steerable by design. On the other, "a daily AI brief" is now a free, bundled commodity built on inbox and calendar access that MCPortal will never have. MCPortal has to win on something the briefings don't do well: **persistent, source-transparent panels of the open web** (HN, GitHub, RSS, YouTube) that stay useful without re-prompting.

AI browsers add a warning about novelty. Arc was put into maintenance mode because it was "too different, too much to learn, for too little reward," and many of its core features were used by a tiny minority ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/05/27/mac-browser-arc-being-discontinued-in-favor-of-new-dia-app/)). The company was then sold to Atlassian for $610M ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/04/atlassian-the-browser-company-deal.html)), and Dia was repositioned for the enterprise with the line "we're building a doer, not a browser" ([PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/09/29/dia-browser-doer-not-a-browser-mike-cannon-brookes/)). Perplexity's Comet had "millions" on its waitlist but was made free within months of a paid launch ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/02/perplexity-ai-comet-browser-free-.html)). Opera Neon launched at $19.99/month, opened public early access in December 2025 ([Opera](https://press.opera.com/2025/12/11/opera-opens-public-access-to-opera-neon-its-experimental-agentic-ai-browser/)), and by August 2026 was a free download with an optional paid plan ([ToolChase](https://toolchase.com/tool/opera-neon/)). Paid consumer "post-browser" products have not held up on their own. MCPortal's "post-browser" framing puts it in the same line of products, so its defaults need to deliver value with no learning curve. The new starter packs are the right instinct.

## MCP Apps give MCPortal reach, but no storefront and no billing

The technical channel exists. MCP Apps (SEP-1865) went live in Claude on 2026-01-26, with interactive UI rendered in sandboxed iframes, and is also supported in VS Code, Goose and ChatGPT ([The Register](https://theregister.com/2026/01/26/claude_mcp_apps_arrives)). One MCPortal build can therefore run in several hosts, although display modes differ (VS Code does not support fullscreen or PiP) ([sunpeak](https://sunpeak.ai/blogs/cross-host-testing-mcp-apps/)). The audience is very large: OpenAI reported **1.2B weekly ChatGPT users** at DevDay on 2026-09-29 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/openais-latest-features-take-direct-aim-at-the-app-store-model/)).

Reaching that audience is a different problem. ChatGPT's directory, renamed the "Plugin Directory" in July 2026 and shared with Codex ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/chatgpt-plugins/)), holds about **2,000–2,300 apps**. Claude's directory had **1,375 connectors** in August 2026, 584 of them added in July alone ([Sitter census](https://www.nicolassitter.com/research/mcp-apps-census-2026); [Node8](https://node8.ai/ai-connectors/chatgpt/)). Developers report low adoption. Alpic's chief of staff said "People don't even know that there are apps in the ChatGPT store," and an analyst said "There's no visible success story of any of these apps being a meaningful driver." Developers also get **no usage data** ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/retailers-are-rushing-to-build-ai-apps-its-unclear-if-shoppers-will-use-them/)). Consumer-facing apps that lead the directories (Spotify, Zillow, Canva, Booking) already have large brands. No non-brand consumer MCP app has published traction numbers.

Three structural rules affect MCPortal directly. First, **"custom connectors are never suggested"** by Claude; only directory listings can be ([Claude docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/directory-vs-custom)). Listing requires submitting from a Team or Enterprise org, and MCP App listings need 3–5 screenshots tested at mobile width ([sunpeak](https://sunpeak.ai/blogs/claude-connector-directory-submission/)). Second, Free Claude users get **one custom connector**, and on Team and Enterprise plans **only Owners can add connectors** ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-get-started-with-custom-connectors-using-remote-mcp)). That is why MCPortal is blocked inside locked-down orgs, which matches the project's own testing experience. Third, the hosts provide no way to charge for a subscription. OpenAI's Apps SDK limits checkout approval to physical goods and excludes digital goods and subscriptions ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/build/monetization)). It shut down Instant Checkout on 2026-03-05 because conversion was poor ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jasongoldberg/2026/03/10/why-openais-checkout-retreat-spells-trouble-for-its-commerce-strategy/)), and announced no rev-share at DevDay 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/openais-latest-features-take-direct-aim-at-the-app-store-model/)). Any revenue has to be collected off-platform through MCPortal's own account and OAuth.

The one real reason for optimism is timing. On 2026-09-29 OpenAI announced **in-conversation app suggestions** and "Sign in with ChatGPT" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/openais-latest-features-take-direct-aim-at-the-app-store-model/)). If the model starts suggesting a listed feed app when a user asks "what's new on HN about Rust?", that is a distribution path no RSS reader has ever had. It is a bet on a lever that has only just been announced, not proven demand. The platform also keeps changing: "apps" became "plugins" within seven months of the directory opening, and custom GPTs are being folded in.

## Nobody ships a feed dashboard inside chat, and that cuts both ways

The directly competing MCP servers are numerous but tiny, and all of them return text only. The official MCP Registry has **37,728 unique server names**, and about 248 match feed or news keywords ([Registry API](https://registry.modelcontextprotocol.io/v0/servers)). Yet every open-source RSS or HN MCP found has fewer than 80 stars, and none renders an interactive UI. The closest serious comparable is an incumbent adding MCP to an existing product: Readwise's official remote MCP works in Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor ([Readwise](https://docs.readwise.io/tools/mcp)). Feedly's official MCP targets threat intelligence, not reading ([Feedly](https://feedly.com/new-features/posts/feedly-mcp-server-automate-cti-workflows-with-claude-and-the-feedly-threat-graph)).

| Competitor | Type | Price | Relevance to MCPortal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glance / Homepage / Dashy | Self-hosted panel dashboards | Free, OSS | Closest concept; YAML setup is the pain MCPortal removes |
| Feedly / Inoreader | Hosted RSS readers with AI tiers | $60–90/yr ([Inoreader](https://www.inoreader.com/pricing)) | Own the paying reader; AI is already bundled |
| Readwise Reader | Read-later + RSS, official MCP | $9.99/mo | Strongest incumbent already inside chat hosts |
| ChatGPT scheduled tasks / Gemini Daily Brief | Built-in AI briefings | Free/bundled | Make "daily brief" a commodity |
| Particle | AI multi-source news app | Freemium, about $15M raised ([Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/particle-raises-10-9m-for-its-ai-powered-news-platform/)) | Mass-market AI news, a different audience |
| mcp-hn, feed-mcp, rss-mcp, etc. | Text-only MCP servers | Free | Low adoption, no UI |

MCPortal's MCP Apps dashboard is genuinely different from all of these. Nothing else combines chosen sources, live panels, reader view and saved items inside the chat host. But an empty niche next to a crowded ecosystem is itself a signal. The weak adoption of text-based feed MCPs suggests that "news in chat" has not yet pulled in developers, and Readwise shows that incumbents will add MCP rather than leave the space open.

## Willingness to pay clusters at $5–10 a month and flows to teams

Consumer feed and reading tools sell in a narrow band. At the low end are Reeder at $10/yr and NewsBlur at $36/yr. Feedbin is $50/yr, Feedly Pro $60/yr and Inoreader Pro $90/yr. Readwise sits at the top at **$99.99/yr** ([LabHub](https://labhub.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-rss-readers-read-later-2026-reeder-netnewswire-inoreader-feedbin-miniflux-freshrss-karakeep-deep-dive?lang=en); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/read-it-later-app-pocket-is-shutting-down-here-are-the-best-alternatives/)). Momentum reached about $1M ARR at $3.33–$4.95/month, which implies a small paying share of a large free user base ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/momentum-dashboard-breakdown)). AI no longer earns a premium on its own. Inoreader bundles 1M AI tokens into Pro ([Readless](https://www.readless.app/blog/inoreader-pricing-2026)). The larger money goes to teams: Feedly's threat and market intelligence products and Inoreader Enterprise ([Inoreader](https://www.inoreader.com/pricing/enterprise)).

MCPortal's position makes this harder in two ways. The segment most drawn to it, developers and self-hosters, expects free and open source, as Bonjourr's no-ads, no-investors positioning and HN recommendation threads show ([HN 39613637](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39613637)). Its users are also already paying $20–200/month for the chat host that renders it, so a separate subscription has to justify itself on top of that. Omnivore's end is a warning about the opposite extreme: it never charged, and it closed in an acqui-hire. The planned curate→publish feature (a public page plus an Atom feed) runs into a related issue. Curation-led publishing clearly works when a person's voice is attached; Substack reached **5M paid subscriptions** in March 2025 ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/12/substack-five-million-paid-subscribers-journalist-reporter-newsletter/)). But that value belongs to the curator's audience, not the tool. No citable evidence was found of demand for tools that turn curation into publishing. Publishing is better treated as a sharing and acquisition loop, where each public page advertises MCPortal, than as something people will pay for.

## Verdict: a real niche, an unproven market, a clear wedge

**Is there demand?** Yes for the problem, not yet for this product as a business. The need for a single, algorithm-free place to watch chosen sources has lasted since iGoogle and is growing among technical users, as Glance's growth shows. The need for zero-maintenance setup is the most-cited pain. What is unproven is whether people will open a chat host to use that workspace every day, whether hosts will send users to it, and whether anyone will pay for it. No public data answers those questions for any product in the category. Retention data is missing for every comparable, and install data is missing for every MCP app.

**The most promising wedge** is the technical power reader who already spends the working day in Claude, ChatGPT or Codex: developers, ML engineers and researchers who track HN, GitHub releases, arXiv-style RSS, and YouTube channels. For this user, MCPortal is a Glance that sets itself up in one sentence and is already open where they work. That avoids Arc's "switch your browser" cost. Lead with starter packs such as "AI engineer," "Rust dev" and "security researcher." Add OPML import to capture people displaced from Pocket, Netvibes and Omnivore. Present the product as "the agent arranges; sources stay pristine," which suits the anti-summary crowd. The second wedge is team monitoring, a shared portal for competitive or ecosystem tracking. That is where feed-reader money actually goes, and it fits the enterprise marketplace OpenAI just announced. It depends on org admins approving the connector.

**The key risks**, in order of severity, start with **host dependence**. Discovery requires a directory listing, Claude submission requires a Team or Enterprise org, enterprise admins can block installs, and the platforms rename and retire features within months (Pulse, Instant Checkout, "apps" becoming "plugins"). Next is the **lack of any monetization path inside the hosts**: billing must happen off-platform, against a $5–10/month ceiling and free open-source alternatives. Third is **platform substitution**, since OpenAI and Google already ship steerable briefings for free and could add persistent source panels. Fourth is **novelty churn**. Every AI curation launch spiked and then decayed, returning to MCPortal depends on the user opening the host daily, and MCP Apps rendering on Claude mobile could not be confirmed. Last is **source fragility**: Reddit and X API costs and page breakage are the chronic complaint of dashboard users.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question MCPortal should be asking. The question is not "do people want an AI dashboard?" History says mostly no, as the fates of Artifact, Pulse and Arc show. The better question is "will the people who already build Glance dashboards by hand let an agent build one for them inside the tool they already live in?" That is a narrower bet and an easier one to test. It shifts the success metric from signups to **weekly returning portals that the user never had to re-prompt**. OpenAI's own Pulse post-mortem points the same way: engagement lived in user-steered tasks, not in curation the AI chose by itself.

The practical implication is to treat the next six months as a distribution experiment, not a revenue one. Get listed in both directories while the in-chat suggestion mechanism is new and the feed category is empty. Measure whether model-driven suggestions deliver users at all. Keep the product open-source-friendly and portable so it earns the trust of a self-hosting audience that has been burned repeatedly. Charge only where evidence says money exists: teams, and heavy readers at Readwise-level prices. If in-chat discovery fails to deliver users, MCPortal's best future may be as an open-source, Glance-style project with an MCP front end, not as a standalone consumer business.
